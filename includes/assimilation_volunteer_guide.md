# How to follow up with love

This is not admin work. It is shepherding, and it cannot be done in our own
strength — not the warmth, not the patience, and certainly not the healing.

> "Not by might nor by power, but by My Spirit, says the LORD of hosts."
> — Zechariah 4:6

> "I am the vine, you are the branches. He who abides in Me, and I in him,
> bears much fruit; for without Me you can do nothing." — John 15:5

So we go leaning on the Holy Spirit. He knows what this person is carrying.
He knows what they need to hear. Ask Him, then listen — to Him, and to them.

## 1. Prepare your heart

**Pray before you dial.** Not a formality — a handover. Ask the Holy Spirit
to lead the conversation, to give you the right words, and to soften and
prepare the heart on the other end of the line before it even rings.

> "For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to
> say." — Luke 12:12

**Pray for each person by name.** Open their card, read the notes, and say
their name before God. One minute per person changes the whole call.

**Check your own heart before you speak.** You are going as a shepherd, not
as an auditor with a register. There is no place in this work for
impatience, superiority, or the quiet suspicion that they have failed.

> "Shepherd the flock of God which is among you... not by compulsion but
> willingly, not for dishonest gain but eagerly; nor as being lords over
> those entrusted to you, but being examples to the flock."
> — 1 Peter 5:2–3

> "I will seek what was lost and bring back what was driven away, bind up
> the broken and strengthen what was sick." — Ezekiel 34:16

If you are tired, bitter, or in a hurry, that will carry down the line.
Wait, pray, and call when you can be present.

## 2. The heart of the call

**Lead with love, not attendance.** The first thing they hear should tell
them they were missed as a person, not counted as a number.

- Say: "Good evening ma, this is Tunde from Household of David. You have
  been on my heart — I just wanted to check on you and the family."
- Say: "We have missed you in the house. How are you doing, truly?"
- Never open with: "Why have you not been coming?" or "We noticed you
  stopped attending."

**Listen far more than you speak.** Ask open questions — "how has this
season been?", "what has it been like for you?" — then be quiet. The
silence you leave is where the real answer comes.

> "Let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath."
> — James 1:19

**No guilt, no pressure, no condemnation.** Ever. Whatever they tell you,
whatever they have done, whatever they have said about the church.

> "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus."
> — Romans 8:1

**Keep their confidence.** What they tell you belongs in the follow-up
record and, where it is serious, with a pastor — nowhere else. Not in the
department WhatsApp group, not with your friend who also knows them.

**Offer to pray with them, on the call.** Ask: "Can I pray with you before
I drop?" Then pray — short, specific to what they just told you, and out
loud. Many people will name the real thing only once you have prayed.

**Close with blessing and one gentle, specific invitation.** Not "try and
come": "Can I look out for you this Sunday? First service is 8 a.m. Would
it help if someone picked you up?" Then agree the next contact with them,
and set the next-touch date on the card before you save.

## 3. Counsel for specific situations

### They were offended or hurt by someone in church

**Approach:** Let them say all of it without interrupting. Do not defend
the person, the department, or the church — not even gently. Your job here
is to receive the wound, not to balance the account.

**Say:** "I am so sorry that happened to you. That was not the heart of the
family towards you, and I am sorry you carried it alone." You can humbly
apologise on behalf of the family even when you were not there. Then ask:
"Would it help to sit with one of the pastors about it?"

**Avoid:** "I am sure they did not mean it." "You know how people are."
Any sentence that makes them argue for their own pain.

> "If your brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault between you
> and him alone." — Matthew 18:15

> "Bearing with one another, and forgiving one another, if anyone has a
> complaint against another; even as Christ forgave you, so you also must
> do." — Colossians 3:13

> "Be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as
> God in Christ forgave you." — Ephesians 4:32

**Escalate:** any offence involving a leader, money, or anything that could
be abuse or harassment goes to a pastor the same day. Do not try to
mediate it yourself.

### They are busy, have relocated, or now attend elsewhere

**Approach:** Honour the season they are in. A member who has moved to
Abuja or joined a healthy church nearby is not a loss to be recovered — they
are family to be blessed.

**Say:** "Thank you for telling me. We are grateful for the years you gave
here, and we are praying for you in this new place." Ask if there is
anything to pray about. Keep the door open: "You will always have a home
here."

**Avoid:** guilt of any shape, and negotiating. Then log it honestly as
*Relocated* or *Attends another church* so nobody calls them again next
month asking the same thing.

> "Let us consider one another in order to stir up love and good works, not
> forsaking the assembling of ourselves together." — Hebrews 10:24–25

> "I thank my God... being confident of this very thing, that He who has
> begun a good work in you will complete it." — Philippians 1:3–6

### They have backslidden or are struggling in their faith

**Approach:** Grace first, always. Most people who have stepped away
already carry more shame than you could add. They are not expecting a warm
voice — give them one.

**Say:** "There is nothing you could tell me that would make us want you
less." Speak restoration, not repair work. If they open up about sin, do
not probe for detail; point them to Jesus and offer a pastor.

**Avoid:** shock, lectures, verses used as correction, and the word
"backslidden" in the conversation.

> "But when he was still a great way off, his father saw him and had
> compassion, and ran and fell on his neck and kissed him... For this my son
> was dead and is alive again; he was lost and is found."
> — Luke 15:20–24

> "Brethren, if a man is overtaken in any trespass, you who are spiritual
> restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness." — Galatians 6:1

> "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins."
> — 1 John 1:9

> "Return, you backsliding children, and I will heal your backslidings."
> — Jeremiah 3:22

### They are grieving, sick, or in hardship

**Approach:** Comfort before anything else. Do not move to attendance, and
do not move to solutions. Sit in it with them for as long as the call lasts.

**Say:** "I am so sorry. I do not have words for this." Then: "Can I pray
with you now?" Ask one practical question — "who is with you?", "have you
eaten?" — and pass real needs to Charis (welfare) and to a pastor.

**Avoid:** "God knows best", "everything happens for a reason", and any
explanation of their suffering. Silence is kinder than a reason.

> "The LORD is near to those who have a broken heart, and saves such as have
> a contrite spirit." — Psalm 34:18

> "The Father of mercies and God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our
> tribulation, that we may be able to comfort those who are in any
> trouble." — 2 Corinthians 1:3–4

> "Rejoice with those who rejoice, and weep with those who weep."
> — Romans 12:15

**Escalate:** bereavement, hospitalisation, or a family crisis goes to
pastoral care immediately, not at the end of the week.

### Spiritual dryness

**Approach:** Normalise it without excusing it. Dryness is a season, not a
verdict, and it frightens faithful people most. Ask what their time with
God looks like now, gently, and share honestly from your own walk.

**Say:** "That has happened to me too. God is not far from you because the
feeling left." Suggest one small step — a psalm a day, one midweek service
— not a programme.

> "As the deer pants for the water brooks, so pants my soul for You, O God."
> — Psalm 42:1–2

> "He gives power to the weak, and to those who have no might He increases
> strength." — Isaiah 40:29–31

### Doubt and hard questions

**Approach:** Doubt spoken out loud is a form of trust — receive it that
way. Do not win the argument, and never fake an answer.

**Say:** "That is a fair question, and I do not want to give you a cheap
answer. Can I bring someone who can sit with it properly?" Doubt is a
conversation to hand to a pastor, not to close.

> "Lord, I believe; help my unbelief!" — Mark 9:24

> "Thomas answered and said to Him, 'My Lord and my God!'" — John 20:27–28

### Relationship or marriage strain

**Approach:** One ear, one story — you are only ever hearing one side, so
take no side. Do not counsel a marriage on the phone.

**Say:** "Thank you for trusting me with that. This deserves more care than
I can give on a call — may I connect you with a pastor?" Pray for both of
them, by name, with them.

**Avoid:** advice, opinions about their spouse, and repeating any of it.

> "Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ."
> — Galatians 6:2

> "Therefore what God has joined together, let not man separate."
> — Mark 10:9

**Escalate:** any hint of violence, threat, or fear for safety goes to a
pastor at once, the same hour.

### Financial pressure

**Approach:** Hear it without flinching and without promising. Transport
fare is a genuine reason people stop coming, and shame keeps them from
saying so.

**Say:** "Thank you for being honest with me. May I pass this to our
welfare team so they can reach out to you properly?" Never promise an
amount or a decision — Charis decides that, not you.

> "Give us day by day our daily bread." — Luke 11:3

> "And my God shall supply all your need according to His riches in glory by
> Christ Jesus." — Philippians 4:19

### Shame, or feeling unseen and unwanted

**Approach:** This is the quietest reason and the most common. Someone
drifted, nobody called, and the silence confirmed what they feared. Your
call is already the answer — say the thing plainly.

**Say:** "I am sorry we did not reach out sooner. That was our failure, not
yours. You were missed." Name one specific thing about them you or others
value, and follow through on whatever you promise, exactly.

> "Are not two sparrows sold for a copper coin?... Do not fear therefore;
> you are of more value than many sparrows." — Matthew 10:29–31

> "You are the God who sees me." — Genesis 16:13

### Conflict with a leader

**Approach:** Take it seriously and take it upward. Do not investigate, do
not explain the leader's side, and do not ask them to overlook it.

**Say:** "That should not have happened, and it should be heard by someone
who can act on it. May I let a pastor know so they can call you?"

> "Do not receive an accusation against an elder except from two or three
> witnesses." — 1 Timothy 5:19

> "He has shown you, O man, what is good... to do justly, to love mercy, and
> to walk humbly with your God." — Micah 6:8

**Escalate:** always, to a pastor — never to the leader concerned.

## 4. After the call

**Log it honestly and briefly.** Write what was said, not what you felt
about it. Use **Clean up notes** to tidy the wording, then read it before
you save — you stay responsible for every word. Keep confidences respectful
on the page too: enough for the next person to care well, no more.

**Set the next touch** before you leave the card, and keep it.

**Keep praying for them through the week.** Put their name somewhere you
will see it.

**Do what you said you would do.** A forgotten promise costs more than the
call gained.

**Escalate the serious things immediately** — do not wait for a meeting.
Anything involving safety, abuse, self-harm, a crisis, a bereavement, or a
complaint against a leader goes to a pastor the same day.

## 5. Keep yourself filled

You cannot pour from an empty vessel, and this work will find the bottom of
yours. Stay fed.

- **Psalm 23** — the Shepherd you are imitating.
- **John 21:15–17** — "Do you love Me? Feed My sheep."
- **Isaiah 40:11** — "He will gather the lambs with His arm."
- **Galatians 6:9** — "Let us not grow weary while doing good."
- **1 Thessalonians 5:14** — "Comfort the fainthearted, uphold the weak, be
  patient with all."
- **Matthew 11:28–30** — take His yoke; it is the only one that rests you.
