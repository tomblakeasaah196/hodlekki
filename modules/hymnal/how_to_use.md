# The Hymnary — how to use

**Who can open it:** every signed-in member. It is in the sidebar under
Command Center, and `Ctrl K` → "hymn" (or "choir", "sol-fa", "song") finds it.

**What is in it:** **511 hymns**, of which **305 carry the full four-part music**
for the choir — and the songs our own music team adds, which is where the
worship the church sings now lives.

Two modes, one library:

- **Congregation** — just the words, as large as you want them. For morning
  devotions on your own phone, and for church when the screen is down.
- **Choir** — the four-part staff, the tonic sol-fa under it, and playback you
  can slow down and take one part at a time.

---

## 1. Finding a hymn

Tap the hymn number at the top left (or the magnifying glass beside it). Then
whichever is easiest:

- **Type the number.** When the Pastor calls out "Hymn 212", type `212` and
  press Enter. A number always wins over a word.
- **Type what you remember.** A title, or a line from the song — "amazing
  grace", or "how sweet the sound". Both work.
- **Tap a shelf.** Hymns, Communion, Christmas & Advent, Easter, Praise &
  Adoration, Faith & Trust, Comfort & Hope, Funerals, Junior Church and the
  rest. The number beside each shelf is how many songs are on it.
- **Tap "With music"** to see only the songs choir mode can show the notes for.

With nothing typed, the drawer opens on **your favourites** and **what you read
lately**, so during devotions it is usually one tap.

`←` and `→` move to the previous and next song. `/` opens the search.

## 2. Congregation mode

The words, and nothing in the way of them.

- **The top bar** is four things: the hymn you are on (tap it to choose
  another), the **reading mode** — its picture, with *Congregation* or *Choir*
  written small underneath — the search, and **⋯** for everything else: *Share
  as a card*, *Text & display*, *About this hymn*, and the Studio for the music
  team. On a narrow phone the bar shows just the number; the full title is right
  below it.
- **Text size:** tap **⋯ → Text & display**, then one of the six sizes. The
  largest is meant to be readable at arm's length by someone who has left their
  glasses at home.
- **Page:** **Paper** is the default — warm and low-contrast, so it does not
  glare in a dark auditorium. **White** is plain. **Night** is for a dark room.
- **Verse numbers** run into the first line, as they do in a hymn book. A
  **refrain** is set in italic with a line beside it, so you can see at a glance
  what comes round again after every verse.
- **Chords:** **⋯ → Text & display**, and switch on *Show chords above the
  words*. Where a song has no chords the switch is greyed out and says so,
  rather than doing nothing.
- **At the foot of the song:** *Previous* and *Next* at the edges, and between
  them the **heart** and the **share** button.
- **Favourite:** the heart. It fills in once the hymn is marked. Your favourites
  are **private to you** — nobody else can see them, including pastors and
  administrators.
- **About:** **⋯ → About this hymn** — who wrote it, when, its metre and key,
  the Scripture behind it (tap a reference to open it in the Bible), and under
  what right the church holds it.

### Sharing a hymn as a card

Tap the **share** button at the foot of the song, or **⋯ → Share as a card**.
You get a picture of the hymn in the church's colours: the hymn number, the
title, the verses, who wrote it, and the Household of David · Lekki name and
logo. It is made like the Bible's verse cards, with the same six backgrounds.

- **On the card:** it starts with **verse 1 and the refrain**. Tap any verse to
  add it or take it off. The words shrink to fit; if you choose more than one
  picture can hold, the card ends with "…" and the dialog tells you to choose
  fewer verses or switch to *Status / Story*, which is tall and holds more.
- **Background:** Paper, Night, Royal, Sunrise, Forest or Rose.
- **Shape:** *Square* for a WhatsApp or Instagram post, *Status / Story* for a
  status.
- **Share** opens your phone's own share sheet (WhatsApp, Instagram and the
  rest). Where a browser has none, the picture is downloaded instead and you
  share it from your downloads. **Download** always saves it.

**Which songs can be shared.** Every song in the library. Each card carries
the writer's name and, in small faint type at the very foot, a line about the
rights — "Words in the public domain" for the hymns, and a credit to the
copyright holders (with the CCLI song number) for songs the music team added.
A song that has not been published yet cannot be shared.

On a phone the top bar slides away while you read down and comes back the
moment you scroll up.

## 3. Choir mode

Tap the mode button at the top (the picture of people, with *Congregation*
under it) and choose **Choir** — the music note.

### The staff

The real engraved score, four parts on two staves, with verse one under the
melody. It scrolls sideways on a phone and fits the page on a computer.

### The sol-fa

Under the staff, one row per part: **d r m f s l t**, bar by bar, lined up the
way a printed sol-fa sheet lines up.

You never type sol-fa and nobody has to transcribe it — it is worked out from
the notes on the staff, so the two can never disagree.

How to read it:

| What you see | What it means |
| ------------ | ------------- |
| `d r m f s l t` | the seven degrees — **doh** is the key note |
| `d'` | the same degree an octave **higher** |
| `d,` | the same degree an octave **lower** |
| `fe` `se` `le` `re` `de` | that degree **sharpened** |
| `ta` `la` `sa` `ma` `ra` | that degree **flattened** |
| a blank | a rest |
| `-` | the note before it, held on (a tie) |

The heading says **doh = G** (or whatever the key note is). One doh serves the
whole song, so the melody reads plainly and the lower parts carry commas —
which means a syllable means the same pitch in every part, and the choir can
read the harmony together.

### Rehearsing one part

The playback bar floats at the foot of the screen: **Play**, the parts, and an
options button that shows the speed (and the key, when you have moved it — for
example `90% · +2`).

- **Tap S, A, T or B** to take just that part. It plays alone and the other
  rows dim on the page. Tap more than one to hear two together. Tap **All** to
  come back.
- **Play** is the round button; the space bar does the same.
- Tap the **options button** for the rest:
  - **Speed** slows the music down to half or pushes it to half again as fast.
    "90%" means the same thing on every song.
  - **Key** — see below.
  - **Sound** is the piano or voices.
  - **Show the sol-fa under the staff** switches the sol-fa rows off and on.

### Changing the key

**Key** (in the options) moves the song up or down by up to five semitones — for a congregation
that cannot reach the written key, or a soloist who needs it lower. The staff
is redrawn in the new key and the playback follows.

**The sol-fa does not change, and that is the point.** Sol-fa names degrees, not
pitches, so `d m s` is `d m s` in every key. Only the "doh = …" heading moves.
The choir reads exactly the same syllables in the new key.

### When there is no score

Choir mode says so, in those words, and the playback and voice buttons are not
there at all — rather than a play button that does nothing. The words below are
still complete, and the music team can add a score in the Studio.

Of the 511 hymns, 305 have the music. Most songs the music team adds will start
with words only.

## 4. Languages

Where a song has more than one set of words — the English and the Yoruba, for
instance — a **Language** choice appears in **⋯ → Text & display**. Both sets sit against the
same song and the same tune, so the choir reads one score whichever words are
being sung.

The 511 hymns are in English. Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa and Pidgin versions are added
by the music team in the Studio, and a language only appears in the interface
once there is something in it.

## 5. The Studio — adding a song

**Who has it:** the **River of Life** department and the pastors. If you help
with music and do not see the Studio button, ask the church office to add you
to River of Life. **Publishing** — making a song visible to the congregation —
is a pastor or a Super_Admin: the music team prepares, a pastor releases.

Tap **⋯** at the top right, then **Studio** (under *Music team*). There are two ways to get a song in, and they
end at the same form.

### Let it be read for you

- **Photograph the song sheet** (or drop in a PDF) — tap the box, or drag a file
  onto it. JPG, PNG, WebP or PDF, under 6 MB.
- **Or paste the words** into the box and tap *Read this for me*.

It takes up to a minute. What comes back lands **in the form, not in the
library**: the title, writer, key, verses and any chords, filled in for you to
check.

**Read it through before you save.** Above the form is a note about what the
reading was unsure of — text it could not make out, a verse that seemed to
continue past the page, a section whose label it had to choose. Check the verse
order and the spelling, especially in Yoruba. Nothing is ever stored, published
or shown to anybody until you press **Save**.

If there is no Gemini API key configured, this half is hidden and says so.
Typing a song in by hand always works.

### Type it in yourself

Tap **Type a song in myself**. The same form, empty.

- **The words:** one block per verse or chorus, one line of the song per line.
  **Verse**, **Chorus** and **Bridge** add another block.
- **Shelves:** at least one, so people can browse to it.
- **Title, writer, key, time, speed, themes, Scripture:** fill in what you know.

### The licence — not optional

Every song has to say how the church is allowed to use it, because the library
mixes public-domain hymns with songs held under the church's copyright licence.
"It was already in the system" is not a licence.

| Choose | When |
| ------ | ---- |
| **Public domain** | Out of copyright — most old hymns |
| **Church copyright licence** | Held under the church's CCLI licence. Needs the **CCLI song number** and **whose licence** it is |
| **Written for this church** | Written by one of our own; the church holds it |
| **Written permission** | You have the writer's permission in writing. Say where it came from |
| **Creative Commons BY / BY-SA** | Say where it came from |

A CCLI song will not publish until **whose licence** is named. We are a branch:
the licence is held by the main church, so name it that way — for example
*"Household of David, main church"*. If the licence is ever queried, it is then
clear whose it is.

### Adding the music

Open **Add the music** at the bottom of the form.

Paste the music as **ABC notation**, one voice per part, then:

1. **Check the music** — the staff is drawn there and then, and it tells you how
   many parts it found and what it called them. Fix the ABC until that looks
   right.
2. **Save the music** — the sol-fa is worked out on the server and shown back to
   you. That is the sol-fa your choir will read, so look at it.

Two staves of two voices, or four staves of one voice each, are recognised as
Soprano / Alto / Tenor / Bass. Anything else is labelled Part 1, Part 2 and so
on — we would rather leave a part unnamed than label a tenor line "Alto" and
have somebody rehearse the wrong notes.

**Remove the music** takes the score off. Choir mode then says the notes are not
available, and the words stay.

### Saving and publishing

- **Save** stores it as a **draft**. A draft already has its permanent hymn
  number, so you can put it on a rehearsal sheet before it is published.
- **Publish to the church** makes it findable by every member. Pastors only.
- **Our songs** lists everything the music team has added, drafts included. Tap
  one to edit it.

A hymn number is **permanent and never reused**, even after a song is retired.
It gets read out from the pulpit and printed on service sheets, so nothing is
ever renumbered. The 511 hymns hold numbers 1–1999; songs the Studio adds start
at 2000.

## 6. Where the hymns come from

The 511 hymns are generated from two public-domain sources and committed to the
repository, never typed in by hand:

- **The Open Hymnal Project** (openhymnal.org) — the four-part scores, with
  every verse.
- **WorshipCommons** (ChurchApps) — the catalogue, the chord charts, and a
  per-part record of each song's copyright status.

Nothing enters that library unless its **words**, its **tune** and its
**harmony** are each, separately, public domain. A hymn being old is not enough:
a modern four-part setting of an old hymn is still somebody's copyright. Seven
recognisable songs are held back because their tune's status was not recorded —
they can be added through the Studio with a licence of their own.

Five scores were refused for a subtler reason worth knowing about: a published
score and a published chord chart can carry the **same tune under different
words**. The chart for "Come, Thou Almighty King" and the score filed with it
share a tune, but the score's words are a different hymn. Those songs keep their
words and say the notes are not available, rather than printing the wrong words
under the staff.

`includes/hymnal/corpus/meta.php` lists every song held back and every score
refused, with the reason.

## 7. For the church office

### Switching it on

The 511 hymns are readable the moment the files are deployed — they are files,
not database rows, so nothing waits on a migration. Favourites, saved reading
positions and the Studio switch on when
`db/migrations/20261120090000_hymnal_core.sql` has run, which happens on the
next deploy. Until then the page says so.

### Who gets the Studio

Add the person to the **River of Life** department (Departments → River of Life)
and they have it on their next page load. Roles are read live, so there is no
need to sign out and back in. Removing them from the department removes it
again.

### The AI reader

Needs `GEMINI_API_KEY` in `.env`. The models fall back to Special Events'
settings and then to `gemini-2.5-flash`, so there is usually nothing to set.

It can be switched off without a deploy, and it never decides anything: a
reading becomes a song only when a person has checked it and pressed Save, and
the record keeps which reading each song came from and who accepted it. No
member's name, phone number or any other personal detail is ever sent — the
only thing that goes out is the song.

Each person may run 20 readings an hour.

### Adding a shelf

Shelves are code, not data: edit `HYMNAL_COLLECTIONS` in
`includes/hymnal/collections.php` and rebuild the corpus. No migration needed.

### Rebuilding the hymn library

Only if the sources change. From the repository root:

```bash
node bin/hymnal_parse_abc.mjs <abc-dir> <source>/parsed
php  bin/build_hymnal.php --source=<source> --verbose
php  tests/hymnal/run.php
```

Then commit everything under `includes/hymnal/corpus/`, including `MANIFEST`
and `NUMBERS`. **Never edit a corpus file by hand** — `MANIFEST` holds a
SHA-256 of each one and the test suite fails on any difference. `NUMBERS` is
append-only: it is what makes a hymn number permanent.

---

## 8. Privacy

Your favourites, where you stopped reading, and your text and page settings are
**private to you**. Every query is scoped to your own account, and there is no
screen anywhere that shows one member's favourites to anybody else. There is no
plan to add one.
